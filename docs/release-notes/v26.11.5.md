## EthernetTools 26.11.5

### Improved: Topology map, which switch first

- **Every device that answers is placed behind the right switch.** Just before reading the switches' MAC tables,
  the Topology window asks every device once more (ARP, and a DCP Identify). Each answer passes the switches on its
  way to this PC, so their tables are fresh. A device that answered but is missing from a switch's table didn't
  send its answer through that switch, so it is on this PC's side of it. It is placed behind that switch, with its
  port shown where a table or LLDP tells it. On our office network, "Not located" went from about 20 devices to
  none.
- **Devices the scan missed are added** when they answer now, the default gateway among them.

### New: SNMPv3

- **SNMPv3...** on the Topology window's toolbar takes an SNMPv3 user: user name, authentication (MD5, SHA-1 or
  SHA-256) and privacy (AES-128). It is asked before v2c and v1, kept in memory until EthernetTools closes, and
  never saved.
- A switch that only speaks SNMPv3 is named as such in the orange warning when no user is given. A switch that
  refuses the user says why: unknown user name, wrong password, and so on.

### New: Profinet's own records

- A Profinet device that doesn't answer SNMP is asked through Profinet itself, the way PRONETA does: each port's
  record (PDPortDataReal) names the neighbour on that port. Its links are drawn on the map, and its box says they
  were read through Profinet.

### Notes

- `EthernetTools.exe --selftest` now also checks SNMPv3 against the RFC 3414 key vectors and the NIST AES vector,
  a whole SNMPv3 exchange with a small agent of its own, and the Profinet record read against a simulated device.
- SNMPv3 has been checked against our switches up to the user check, but not yet with a real user. The Profinet
  record read hasn't been tried on a real Profinet IO device yet.
