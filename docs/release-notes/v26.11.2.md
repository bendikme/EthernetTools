## EthernetTools 26.11.2

### New: Topology map

**Topology...** on the toolbar draws a map of the scanned network: switches, routers and devices, with the
physical port at each end of every cable. Scan first, then open it.

The map is built the way PRONETA and TIA Portal build theirs. Traceroute only sees routers, not switches or
ports, so the map uses three other sources:
- **LLDP:** Profinet devices and managed switches announce, on each port, who is plugged in there. EthernetTools
  listens on its own adapter to learn which switch port this PC is plugged into.
- **SNMP:** EthernetTools reads each device's LLDP neighbours (read-only, community `public` unless you change
  it). From switches it also reads the MAC address table. That table places devices that don't speak LLDP, such
  as PCs and printers, on their port. Several devices behind one port with no LLDP neighbour there show as an
  "Unmanaged switch?".
- **The default gateway** is shown as a router.

Devices that can only be placed somewhere behind a switch EthernetTools can't read show as "port ?". Many such
devices are drawn as one group box. Devices it can't place at all are listed under "Not located".

In the map, solid lines are cables known from LLDP at both ends, dotted lines come from a switch's MAC table,
and orange dashed lines close a ring (for example a Profinet MRP ring). The mouse wheel zooms, dragging moves
the map, hovering a box shows its details, and double-clicking a box selects that device in the list. **Fit**
shows the whole map, and **Export PNG...** saves it as a picture.

### Notes

- Switches that don't answer SNMP, or not to community `public`, still appear through their neighbours'
  LLDP, but the map can't see which of their ports each device is on.
- `EthernetTools.exe --selftest` now also checks the SNMP encoding, LLDP frames, the LLDP and bridge tables,
  and a whole map built from an example network.
